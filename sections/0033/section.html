01 of twenty-one This one went wrong

The call

Forty minutes. You talk, I take notes. No slide deck, no discovery fee, no three week strategy phase. At the end of it I know what you sell, who buys it, and what you are tired of explaining on the phone.

What I found

Your current site, your Google listing, and the three businesses that show up above you. Sent to you as a plain list of what is wrong and what it is costing, not as a branded PDF with a cover page.

The plan, on one page

Every page, what it says, and why it exists. You read it and approve it before a single line of code is written. If something on that page does not earn its place, it comes off now instead of after you have paid for it.

A version you can actually open

Live on a real address, on your phone, in your hand. Not a picture of a website in a presentation. You can show it to your spouse and your best customer and tell me what is wrong with it.

It was too slow, and that was on me

Your gallery photos were four megabytes each and I had shipped them straight through. On a phone away from wifi the page took six seconds. I rebuilt how images load and it came down to under one. You should never have had to know that number existed.

Your words, not mine

We read every line on the site out loud on a call. Anything that did not sound like something you would actually say got cut and rewritten. This is the step most people skip, and it is why most small business sites read like they were written by nobody.

Making sure you can be found

Google Business Profile, the site, and every directory listing made to agree on one address, one phone number, one set of hours. Search engines treat disagreement as doubt, and doubt costs you the map result.

You broke the contact form on purpose

You asked what happens if somebody hits send with an empty message. It sent. An empty email, no name, no way to call them back. A real customer would have thought they had contacted you and then waited. Fixed that afternoon, and I now test it that way on every build.

Live, and you hold the keys

Domain registered in your name. Hosting on your account. Every password handed over in writing. My mobile number in your phone. If you ever want to leave, you take all of it with you and you do not need my permission.
